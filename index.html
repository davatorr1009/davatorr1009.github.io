About

I'm a mechatronics engineer from Guayaquil, Ecuador (B.Sc., ESPOL 2025). Most of my work sits where hardware meets collective behaviour: designing robots that are cheap and reliable enough to be built in numbers, and the simulation tools to test them before they are.

I want to spend a PhD learning to design, and experimentally validate, the behaviours that make robot swarms useful outside the lab.

Publications

ROBOT 2026 · 9th Iberian Robotics Conference, Barcelona · Accepted

Learn alone or learn together? A minimal model of social learning in robot swarms

G. Madroñero Pachajoa*, D. Torres Torres*, A. Tutivén Gálvez, J. Santos Terán, C. Samaniego Torres, P. S. Padrón Martínez, B. Puruncajas, C. Tutivén Gálvez, C. Saldarriaga, D. Garzón Ramos *Equal contribution

We extend a minimal task-allocation model so that each robot improves not only by repeating a task, but also by watching nearby peers perform it. With eight QUPA robots, physical and simulated in ARGoS, social learning increased the number of tasks the swarm completed.

My part: I was responsible for the physical-robot experiments, validating the model in the real world with a swarm of eight QUPA robots.

The QUPA swarm robot

CoRAL Lab, ESPOL · 2024–now

QUPA is CoRAL's low-cost robot for swarm robotics research and the platform of our social-learning paper. I worked on every iteration after the first prototype: the main board in EasyEDA, review of the mechanical design and its SolidWorks documentation, and now its omnidirectional vision.

Omnidirectional vision in progress

I stream the camera over ROS 2 on the robot's headless Ubuntu Server, and I am improving the mirror so that more of the image shows the arena. Starting from a real capture of the current mirror, I use ray-traced Blender renders to compare profiles: the current hyperbolic mirror (A), a spherical one (B) and a custom profile (C), which devotes far more of the image to neighbouring robots.

Swarm robotic manipulation

B.Sc. thesis · CoRAL Lab, ESPOL · 2024–2025

I designed a five-bar parallel SCARA manipulator for QUPA, CoRAL's differential-drive swarm robot, so that several robots can pick up and carry objects together. The work covered the mechanism, a modular electronics board, vision-based object detection and the integration with the robot, all fabricated by additive manufacturing.

All results below come from simulation; testing with multiple physical robots is the next step.

Navigation among people

Research internship (remote) · RISE Lab, Sungkyunkwan University, with HD Hyundai Robotics · 2024

In a project with HD Hyundai Robotics and under Prof. Nabih Pico, I built a ROS 2 and Gazebo pipeline to test mobile-robot navigation in scenes with moving people: SLAM and AMCL localisation, a Nav2 stack, and a benchmark of local controllers (DWA, MPC, MPPI and learning-based variants) across five scenarios.

ROS Noetic · ROS 2 Humble · Gazebo Classic & Fortress · Python · C++

Autonomous racing

Roboracer Ecuador · 1st place · 2025

I led ESPOL's AIRoS team to first place in Roboracer Ecuador, racing 1/10-scale autonomous cars built on a Jetson Orin Nano, a 2D LiDAR and a VESC motor controller. A second AIRoS team finished second.

I worked on the full autonomy stack (mapping, localisation, path planning and control) and coordinated the team's hardware work.
